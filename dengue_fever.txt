Dengue spreads through the bites of Aedes mosquitoes, which bite mostly during the day and breed in clean standing water. Symptoms usually include sudden high fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pain, nausea and sometimes a rash. Most people recover with rest and plenty of fluids. Doctors commonly advise paracetamol for fever and avoiding aspirin and ibuprofen because of bleeding risk. Warning signs often appear around days 3 to 7 as the fever falls and need urgent hospital care: severe stomach pain, repeated vomiting, bleeding from gums or nose, black stools, extreme tiredness, restlessness and fast breathing. Prevention means removing standing water from containers, coolers and tyres, using repellents and wearing full sleeves.
